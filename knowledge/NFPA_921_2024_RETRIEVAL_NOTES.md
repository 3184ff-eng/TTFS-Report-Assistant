# NFPA 921 2024 Retrieval Notes

Source: `NFPA 921 - 2024.docx`, supplied by the user.

Use these notes as concise retrieval guidance for the TTFS Report Assistant. These notes do not replace the full source standard/guide and do not override TTFS report rules.

## Core Use in the App

NFPA 921 supports a systematic, safe, evidence-based investigation or analysis of fire and explosion incidents. Use it to guide:

- Scientific method workflow
- Scene safety
- Data collection and evidence preservation
- Fire effects and fire pattern interpretation
- Origin determination
- Cause determination
- Electrical system analysis
- Documentation and reporting

## Scientific Method

Fire investigation should follow a scientific-method sequence:

1. Recognize the need.
2. Define the problem.
3. Collect data.
4. Analyze data.
5. Develop hypotheses.
6. Test hypotheses.
7. Select the final hypothesis.

If an app answer cannot complete one of these steps from officer-provided facts, it must flag the missing step or data gap.

## Origin and Cause

For origin analysis, evaluate fire effects, fire patterns, fuel packages, ventilation, smoke and heat flow, building/room characteristics, witness data, fire service actions, electrical activity, and reconstruction information.

For cause analysis, distinguish:

- Area of origin
- Point or specific area of origin where supported
- Ignition source
- First fuel or material ignited
- Oxidant
- Ignition sequence
- Circumstances that brought the ignition source, fuel, and oxidant together

Do not state a final origin or cause unless supported by available facts and tested against competing hypotheses.

## Fire Effects and Fire Patterns

Fire effects and fire patterns should be interpreted in context, not in isolation. Consider:

- Heat, smoke, and flame movement
- Lines or areas of demarcation
- Clean burn and soot deposition
- Depth of char or material loss
- Melting, deformation, oxidation, spalling, calcination, and thermal expansion
- Fuel-controlled versus ventilation-controlled burning
- Suppression and overhaul effects
- Items moved during fireground operations or investigation

Avoid single-indicator conclusions, especially where ventilation, suppression, or post-fire disturbance could explain the same pattern.

## Electrical Fire Analysis

Electrical analysis may include building electrical systems, services, panels, breakers, receptacles, appliances, wiring, arc mapping, and examination of conductors.

Important safeguards:

- Do not assume electrical causation solely from melted conductors.
- Distinguish cause arcs from victim arcs and exposure damage.
- Consider whether electrical damage is a cause, effect, or unrelated artifact.
- Document electrical evidence before removal.
- Maintain scene safety and lock-out/tag-out awareness where electrical systems remain hazardous.

## Building Systems and Fire Protection Systems

Where relevant, document:

- Building construction and room characteristics
- Heating, ventilation, air-conditioning, fuel gas, and utility systems
- Active fire protection systems
- Fire alarm, sprinkler, suppression, security, and life safety systems
- Design, installation, maintenance, operating status, and event data where available

These items may affect fire development, detection, suppression, origin analysis, and cause analysis.

## Scene Safety

Use a safety plan that identifies hazards, PPE needs, structural instability, electrical hazards, chemical exposure, atmosphere hazards, biological hazards, sharp objects, confined spaces, and post-fire contamination.

If safety information is missing, ask for it or flag it before recommending scene actions.

## Sources of Information

Potential non-scene and scene information includes:

- Witnesses and first responders
- Dispatch, call, and communications records
- Photographs and video
- CCTV and digital systems
- Weather
- Utility records
- Building plans and permits
- Fire protection system records
- Laboratory results
- Outside reports
- Product and manufacturer data

Do not rely on a single source when other available sources can corroborate or contradict it.

## Documentation and Reporting

Document the investigation with notes, photographs, sketches, diagrams, evidence records, witness information, timeline data, and report narratives.

For TTFS reports:

- Put direct observations from arrival to departure in Officer's Observations.
- Put investigation findings, witness statements, received information, and pre-arrival actions in Additional Information or internal investigation notes.
- Preserve missing/unknown/unconfirmed information rather than guessing.
