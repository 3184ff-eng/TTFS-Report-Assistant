# NFPA 1033 2022 Retrieval Notes

Source: `NFPA  1033 2022 edition.docx`, supplied by the user.

Use these notes to guide the Fire Investigation AI Agent on fire investigator competency and job performance expectations. These notes do not replace the full source standard and do not override TTFS report rules.

## Core Use in the App

NFPA 1033 identifies minimum job performance requirements for personnel who perform or support fire investigations. In the app, use it to prompt for completeness and professional competence across:

- Scene examination
- Scene documentation
- Evidence recognition, collection, and preservation
- Interviews
- Post-incident investigation
- Report preparation
- Presentation of findings

## Scene Examination

The agent should prompt the officer to examine and document:

- Fire scene safety hazards
- Fire patterns and fire effects
- Building construction
- Fire protection systems
- Utilities and electrical systems
- Fuel packages and ventilation
- Possible origin areas
- Possible ignition sources
- Explosion indicators where relevant

If a required examination element is not provided, flag it as missing or requiring officer review.

## Documenting the Scene

Documentation should be sufficient for later review. Prompt for:

- Notes
- Photographs
- Sketches or diagrams
- Measurements
- Evidence location records
- Scene orientation
- Witness viewpoint documentation
- Fire spread and damage documentation

The agent should not invent documentation that was not performed or provided.

## Evidence Collection and Preservation

Evidence handling should identify:

- Type of evidence
- Location found
- Condition
- Photograph reference
- Collection method
- Packaging/storage method
- Chain-of-custody information
- Whether evidence is critical to the investigation

Missing chain-of-custody or evidence-location details must be flagged.

## Interviews and Source Information

Evaluate and correlate source information from first responders, witnesses, owners, occupiers, discoverers, and other available sources.

Interview-related guidance should preserve source identity and uncertainty:

- Who provided the information
- How they know it
- Whether the information is direct observation, hearsay, received information, or documentary/digital data
- Whether other facts corroborate or contradict it

For TTFS reports, witness statements and received information belong in Additional Information, not Officer's Observations.

## Post-Incident Investigation and Reporting

The app should support:

- File assessment
- Accurate documentation
- Report evaluation
- Data correlation
- Chain-of-custody implementation
- Identification of reports/documents needed for the investigation
- Clear communication of findings and limitations

Reports should separate facts, analysis, conclusions, and unresolved gaps.
