# Redsicker Practical Fire and Arson Investigation Retrieval Notes

Source: `David R. Redsicker - Practical Fire and Arson Investigation, Second Edition (1996).pdf`, supplied by the user.

Use these notes as practical scene and arson-investigation retrieval guidance. Do not copy long passages from the source into generated reports.

## Core Use in the App

This source supports:

- Practical fire scene examination
- Arson motive awareness
- Building construction and fire problems
- Fire chemistry and behavior
- Determining origin and cause
- Eliminating accidental causes
- Fatal fire investigation considerations
- Fire spread, burn patterns, smoke/flame observations, and suppression effects

## Scene Examination

Prompt for:

- Exterior examination
- Interior examination
- Room or area of origin
- Point of origin where supported
- Fire language: pattern, spread, suppression, ventilation, smoke, heat, and flame observations
- Building construction and fire protection systems
- Fuel load and fire spread routes

## Eliminating Accidental Causes

When assessing cause, consider and document whether facts support or eliminate:

- Electrical fires
- Lightning
- Gas fires
- Fuel oil fires
- Heating equipment
- Cooking-related fires
- Open flames and sparks
- Children, pets, or human activity
- Improper storage of combustibles or flammables
- Smoking materials
- Construction, renovation, or demolition activity
- Product/equipment failure

Do not classify a fire as incendiary simply because accidental causes are not yet documented. Missing elimination data should be flagged.

## Arson and Incendiary Caution

When incendiary cause is considered, require supporting facts such as credible multiple origin evidence, ignitable liquid evidence, reliable witness/digital data, access/opportunity, or other corroborated indicators. Keep motive information separate from physical origin/cause facts.
