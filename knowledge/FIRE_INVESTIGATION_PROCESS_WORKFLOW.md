# Fire Investigation Process Workflow

Source: `_Fire Investigation Process Map.pdf`, OSAC Fire & Explosion Investigation Subcommittee Fire Investigation Process Map, current practice, March 14, 2023.

Use this workflow to guide fire investigation support in the TTFS Report Assistant. The workflow is a process guide, not a substitute for TTFS policy, officer judgment, lawful authority, or the official TTFS Fire Report Form requirements.

## 1000 Pre-scene Activities

- Receive assignment and establish the need for investigative services.
- Gather initial information: date of loss, fire location, property type and scope, extent of damage, known hazards, hazardous materials, injuries or fatalities, jurisdiction, parties involved, and who is in charge.
- Identify permission/access issues, interested parties, partner agencies, media reports, equipment needs, and possible pre-scene interviews.
- Conduct safety assessment, identify hazards, determine PPE needs, prepare a safety plan, document safety concerns, and brief personnel where required.

## 1200 Scene Access

- Confirm lawful access before entering the scene.
- Document whether access is by consent, agency authority, exigent circumstances, search warrant, administrative warrant, court order, or another approved route.
- If access is delayed, document permitted tasks completed while awaiting access, such as photographs from a distance, interviews, or checks for neighbouring security video.

## 1400-1500 Witness Information

- Identify and document fact witnesses and incident-related witnesses.
- Document contact details, interview timing, interview method, staged or follow-up interviews, and whether interviews occur with a walk-through.
- Separate witness statements, received information, and pre-arrival information from Officer's Observations.
- For digital witness information, record device/system type, file or storage type, person in possession, and how information was collected.

## 2000 Data Collection

- Data collection is ongoing throughout the investigation.
- Document general observations, including odour, sound, visual observations, ventilation sources, building and room characteristics, dimensions, fuels, contents, moved items, lining materials, disturbed areas, and building systems.
- Document fire effects, suppression effects, overhaul effects, forced entry, ventilation changes, utility disconnections, and items moved by crews or investigators.
- Preserve evidence and samples with chain of custody where required.
- Collect scene and non-scene data, including maps, CCTV, weather, utility status, dispatch data, communication logs, injuries, outside reports, research, and laboratory or reconstruction results.

## 3000-3200 Origin Determination

- Analyze fire effects, fire patterns, fire dynamics, witness information, evidence, timeline, and reconstruction data.
- Assign meaning to patterns in context, including fuel-controlled versus ventilation-controlled patterns and uncertainty in data points.
- Develop all area-of-origin hypotheses supported by the data.
- Test hypotheses against analyzed case data, scientific knowledge, fire dynamics, experiments, modeling, literature, timelines, and other appropriate methods.
- Document eliminated hypotheses, surviving hypotheses, uncertainty, and need for additional data.

## 4000-4300 Cause Determination

- Identify all potential ignition sources, first fuels, and oxidants in the area of origin.
- Build and update the fire-event timeline from data, analyses, witness information, and identified patterns.
- Develop ignition-sequence hypotheses based on ignition source, fuel, oxidant, and circumstances that brought them together.
- Test each hypothesis for competence of ignition source, proximity, temperature, duration, timeline consistency, and fit with all observations and data.
- Cause determination must identify, where supported, ignition source, first material ignited, oxidant, and ignition sequence.
- TTFS official cause classification must remain limited to Natural, Accidental, Incendiary, or Undetermined.

## 5000-5100 Reporting and Review

- Prepare report or progress report as required.
- Report content may include scope, data collection, data analysis, origin conclusion, cause conclusion, classification, methodology, scene and non-scene data, witness information, hypotheses, testing, conclusions, references, personnel, signatures, and appendices.
- Conduct technical and administrative review where required.
- Resolve review issues through consultation, further analysis, additional data, revisions, documented disagreement, or agency escalation.
- Archive case record, including files, data, analysis, hypotheses, documentation, findings, and issued reports.

## TTFS Placement Rules

- Officer's Observations must contain only what the officer directly observed from arrival to departure.
- Witness statements, information received, pre-arrival actions, access/legal notes, investigation-process notes, and uncertain information belong in Additional Information or internal investigation notes.
- Do not invent origin or cause facts. If an element is missing, unknown, unconfirmed, or requires officer review, flag it clearly.
- Do not convert probable cause language into a TTFS cause classification unless the classification is explicitly provided or confirmed by the officer.
