# Davis Liquid Versus Vapor Propane Retrieval Notes

Source: `2018Proceedings_Davis - Liquid_v_Vapor.pdf`, supplied by the user.

Use these notes for LPG/propane flash fire and explosion analysis. Do not quote long passages from the source into generated reports.

## Core Use in the App

This paper is useful when an incident involves:

- LPG or propane cylinders
- Gas or liquid propane leak hypotheses
- Flash fire or explosion
- Leak testing with nitrogen or inert gas
- Orifice flow assumptions
- Claims about liquid propane releases being dramatically larger than vapor releases
- CFD/modeling or quantitative leak-rate analysis

## Key Investigation Caution

Do not accept a simple density-ratio claim as proof of leak magnitude. A liquid propane leak should not automatically be treated as hundreds of times larger than a vapor leak through the same hole.

For practical investigation guidance, prompt for:

- Cylinder size and fill state
- Cylinder temperature and pressure
- Hole/orifice size and location relative to liquid level
- Whether the release was liquid or vapor
- Leak-test medium and pressure
- Measured leak rate
- Valve/cap condition
- Ventilation and accumulation conditions
- Ignition source location and timing
- Whether modeling or calculations support the claimed fuel quantity

## Hypothesis Testing

For propane/LPG hypotheses, require:

- Leak path evidence
- Flow-rate estimate
- Accumulation/dispersion analysis
- Ignitable concentration assessment
- Ignition source competence
- Timeline consistency
- Alternative explanations

Flag unsupported statements about propane accumulation or leak size.
